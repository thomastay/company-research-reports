# IQVIA Holdings (NYSE: IQV) Research Primer

**Research cutoff:** September 23, 2026  
**Latest reported period:** Quarter ended June 30, 2026  
**Market-data date:** September 22, 2026 close  

IQVIA is best classified as a **mature growth compounder in a cyclical recovery**, not a turnaround. It combines one of the world's largest contract research organizations with a scaled life-sciences data, analytics, technology, and commercialization platform. TTM revenue is growing about 8%, profitability and free cash flow remain positive, and clinical bookings have recovered. The central investment debate is whether proprietary healthcare data, integrated workflows, and global clinical scale can sustain mid-single-digit or better organic growth while high leverage, AI disruption, clinical cancellations, and data-rights risk remain controlled.

## 1. Business Overview & Revenue Model

### Core Business: What does the company do, and how exactly does it make money?

IQVIA serves pharmaceutical, biotechnology, medical-device, payer, provider, and other healthcare clients across the product life cycle:

- **R&D Solutions** runs clinical trials and related development work, including full-service studies, functional service provider arrangements, laboratories, site and patient services, and selected real-world evidence activities. Contracts can last several years. Revenue is generally recognized as work is performed, while billing may follow milestones. Reported revenue includes reimbursed investigator, travel, and other pass-through costs that carry little or no profit.
- **Commercial Solutions** sells healthcare information, data licenses, technology platforms, analytics, consulting, real-world evidence, patient services, commercialization support, and outsourced sales and medical teams. These offerings range from recurring data/software subscriptions to projects and labor-based services.

IQVIA changed its reporting structure on January 1, 2026. Former Contract Sales & Medical Solutions, or CSMS, moved into Commercial Solutions, while $674 million of selected late-phase real-world offerings moved from the former Technology & Analytics Solutions, or TAS, segment into R&D Solutions. The change better aligns reporting with how customers buy, but it complicates historical comparisons. See the [2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1478242/000162828026008322/iqv-20251231.htm), [FY2025 results and recast tables](https://ir.iqvia.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2026/IQVIA-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Results-Issues-Full-Year-2026-Guidance/default.aspx), and [Q2 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1478242/000162828026050211/iqv-20260630.htm).

The commercial engine rests on unusually broad healthcare data and delivery infrastructure. IQVIA reports more than 10,000 clients, approximately 68 petabytes of proprietary data, 150,000 data suppliers, over one million data feeds, and more than 1.2 billion non-identified patient records. It receives roughly 120 billion healthcare records annually, operates in more than 100 countries, and employed approximately 94,000 people at Q2 2026. No customer represented 10% of 2025 revenue; the largest was about 5%. These are company disclosures, not independently audited measures of data uniqueness.

### Unit Economics: Detail the unit economics and the overall scalability of the business model

IQVIA has two different economic models under one roof:

| Economic characteristic | Commercial Solutions | R&D Solutions |
|---|---|---|
| Primary revenue drivers | Data licenses, subscriptions, analytics, consulting, commercialization and patient programs | Trial awards, backlog conversion, project scope, FSP staffing, laboratories and reimbursed costs |
| Scalability | Higher for reusable data, software, algorithms and common infrastructure; lower for custom consulting and field services | Moderate; global scale improves utilization, purchasing, site access and delivery, but execution remains people- and vendor-intensive |
| Revenue visibility | Generally supported by renewals, embedded workflows and recurring data needs | Supported by multi-year backlog, but customers can delay, resize or cancel trials |
| Margin sensitivities | Data-acquisition costs, mix, consulting utilization, software investment and AI | Full-service versus FSP mix, pass-through expenses, labor, site costs, cancellations and fixed-price execution |

H1 2026 segment profit was $800 million on $3.547 billion of Commercial Solutions revenue, a **22.6% segment margin**, and $998 million on $4.972 billion of R&D Solutions revenue, a **20.1% segment margin**. Segment profit excludes corporate costs, depreciation, amortization, and restructuring, so it is not a GAAP operating margin. TTM free cash flow was approximately **$2.184 billion**, or **12.9% of revenue**, after $635 million of capital expenditure. This supports buybacks and acquisitions, but capital deployment has exceeded internally generated cash over some periods.

The key accounting caveat is pass-through revenue. In Q2 2026, R&D Solutions revenue grew **8.8% reported** but **6.7% excluding reimbursed expenses**. Investors should emphasize ex-reimbursed growth, service-fee bookings, margins, and cash generation rather than gross revenue alone. ## 3. Strategic Classification (Growth vs. Turnaround)

IQVIA is a **mature compounder with a cyclical recovery underway**. It is not a turnaround: revenue never entered a sustained decline, GAAP earnings remained positive, free cash flow stayed strong, and backlog continued to expand through the 2023-2025 slowdown. It is also not a high-growth software company; underlying growth is constrained by pharma budgets, trial cycles, labor content, and a large low-single-digit information business.

The recent cycle had two phases:

1. **2023 through early 2025:** large-pharma portfolio reprioritization, trial futility, record cancellations, delayed "mega trials," weak biotech funding, longer decisions, pricing pressure, and a less favorable FSP/full-service mix slowed R&D Solutions.
2. **Mid-2025 through Q2 2026:** RFP flow, win rates, decision times, bookings, and backlog conversion improved. Q2 2026 R&D bookings rose 19% to $3.15 billion; book-to-bill reached 1.22x; backlog reached $34.2 billion; and $9.2 billion was expected to convert over the following 12 months, up 7.5%.

CEO Ari Bousbib's stated growth plan comprises:

- **Integrated molecule-to-market selling:** combine clinical execution, real-world evidence, proprietary data, analytics, technology, and commercialization to win larger enterprise relationships and benefit from vendor consolidation.
- **"See More, Win More":** pursue a broader R&D opportunity set and improve share. Management accepted some pricing pressure in 2025 but said pricing and selectivity normalized by Q3 2025.
- **Healthcare-grade AI:** use regulated healthcare data and domain workflows as the foundation for agents and automation. Management reported 294 agents across 90 use cases by Q2 2026, versus more than 150 agents and 30-plus use cases at Q4 2025. Agent counts are adoption indicators, not disclosed revenue.
- **Broader emerging-biopharma exposure:** management estimated the R&D revenue mix at roughly 50% large pharma, 15% midsize pharma, and 35% emerging biopharma in Q2 2026.
- **Commercial launch and patient-service growth:** Bousbib said H1 2026 launches were approximately 45% above H1 2025. Information, about 30% of Commercial Solutions, is a low-single-digit grower; analytics and consulting, about 20%, grow mid- to high-single digits; patient solutions, technology, and commercial engagement, about 50%, grow high-single to low-double digits.
- **Productivity and selective M&A:** offshoring, labor and organizational optimization, automation, and acquisitions are intended to protect margins and extend the offering.

At the December 2024 Investor Day, management described a **6%-9% medium-term growth framework**: 3%-5% underlying pharma R&D/SG&A growth, approximately one point from outsourcing, at least one point from share gains, and 1%-2% from acquisitions. This is management's framework, not an independent forecast. Sources: [2024 Investor Day transcript](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/iqv/transcripts/266665-investor-day-2024/), [Q4 2025 earnings call](https://www.roic.ai/quote/IQV/transcripts/2025-year/4-quarter), [Q1 2026 call](https://www.roic.ai/quote/IQV/transcripts/2026-year/1-quarter), and [Q2 2026 call](https://www.roic.ai/quote/IQV/transcripts/2026-year/2-quarter).

### A general industry primer

**Clinical research outsourcing.** Sponsors outsource trial design support, regulatory work, site selection, patient recruitment, monitoring, data management, biostatistics, laboratories, and other functions. The market includes full-service projects, FSP staffing, and hybrid models. Buying criteria are therapeutic expertise, enrollment performance, quality, inspection history, global reach, technology, delivery reliability, and price. Multi-year backlog offers visibility, but contracts are cancellable and trial futility can eliminate revenue abruptly. Biotech funding is a leading indicator with an uncertain lag.

**Healthcare data and real-world evidence.** Vendors aggregate claims, prescription, electronic health record, registry, laboratory, consumer, and other data, then normalize and link them for commercial insights, safety, outcomes research, market access, and regulatory evidence. Coverage, provenance, permitted use, refresh speed, representativeness, linkage quality, privacy controls, and scientific fitness determine value. The market is structurally attractive but exposed to data-supplier bargaining power and regulation.

**Life-sciences software and commercial technology.** CRM, content, master data, omnichannel engagement, trial-management, eClinical, and analytics platforms compete on compliance, interoperability, usability, implementation risk, and ecosystem breadth. Veeva is the most important focused software competitor. IQVIA and Veeva resolved litigation and formed commercial and clinical partnerships in 2025, illustrating that the relationship is both competitive and complementary. See the [IQVIA-Veeva partnership announcement](https://www.iqvia.com/newsroom/2025/08/iqvia-and-veeva-announce-long-term-clinical-and-commercial-partnerships).

**Commercial and medical services.** Outsourced field forces, patient-support programs, medical affairs, and launch services are fragmented and often tied to product cycles. Scale helps deployment and compliance, but these services generally have less durable revenue than embedded data and software.

Third-party estimates place the 2025 CRO market near $92 billion ([Fortune Business Insights](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/industry-reports/contract-research-organization-cro-services-market-100864)) and the 2025 RWE solutions market near $3 billion ([Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/real-world-evidence-solutions-market-report)), with high-single-digit long-term growth, but definitions vary substantially. IQVIA itself estimates a $335 billion addressable market, including $159 billion outsourced R&D, $90 billion real-world evidence/connected health, and $84 billion technology-enabled commercial operations. Treat all TAM estimates as directional.

### Moat & Dynamics

IQVIA has a meaningful but imperfect moat:

- **Data scale and specialization:** The breadth, longitudinal depth, normalization, and regulatory handling of IQVIA's healthcare data are costly to replicate. The advantage is strongest where data are embedded in customer decisions and combined with domain expertise.
- **Switching costs:** Commercial master data, analytics, integrations, and workflows can be deeply embedded. Changing CROs mid-trial also carries operational, data-transfer, quality, and regulatory risk. However, CRO contracts can be terminated, customers multi-source, and software migrations remain possible.
- **Global delivery assets:** Global teams, therapeutic experience, site relationships, laboratories, and regulatory capabilities enable large, complex programs. These are specialized capabilities and scale economies, but not unique physical assets in the manner of a railroad or exchange.
- **Cross-sell and platform breadth:** Few competitors span clinical development, real-world evidence, commercial data, analytics, patient services, and field execution at comparable scale. The integrated offer can improve win rates and wallet share.
- **No pure network effect:** More data and usage can improve benchmarks and analytics, but data suppliers often serve multiple vendors and one client's participation does not automatically increase value for all others. The moat is better described as data scale, expertise, embedded workflows, and switching costs than as a textbook network effect.
- **AI can strengthen or weaken the moat:** Proprietary data rights, regulated workflows, and scientific expertise can make IQVIA an attractive AI partner. Conversely, general-purpose models can automate lower-level analytics, coding, consulting, or FSP work, let clients internalize tasks, and reduce differentiation.

### Topical issues from the last two years

Analysts have repeatedly focused on:

- R&D cancellations, trial futility, delayed large trials, and whether backlog is genuinely convertible.
- Quarterly book-to-bill quality, pass-through volatility, and management's view that one quarter's ratio is a poor growth predictor.
- Biotech funding recovery and the lag before funding becomes awards and revenue.
- Pricing pressure under "See More, Win More," and the mix between lower-margin FSP and full-service work.
- Whether AI is incremental revenue and productivity or a cannibalization threat.
- Why Commercial/TAS has been more resilient than R&D Solutions.
- Margin delivery amid wage inflation, pass-through growth, acquisitions, and interest expense.
- IRA, most-favored-nation drug-pricing policy, tariffs, FDA/NIH changes, and their effect on sponsor decisions.
- The preliminary review of inactive trials in backlog. Management estimated about 5% at Q2 2026 and expected to complete the review in Q3.

## 5. Management & Capital Allocation

### Track Record: Evaluate the management team's history and effectiveness

Ari Bousbib has led the combined company since the 2016 Quintiles-IMS merger and remains Chairman and CEO. His tenure offers strategic continuity and the merger produced a differentiated combination of data and clinical services. Revenue grew from $13.874 billion in 2021 to $16.310 billion in 2025, a 4.1% CAGR, while adjusted EBITDA grew from $3.022 billion to $3.788 billion, a 5.8% CAGR. Execution has nevertheless been uneven: constant-currency revenue growth slowed to 3.4% in 2024, 2023 guidance was missed across revenue and adjusted profit, and headline adjusted EBITDA margin contracted in 2025.

Guidance performance has been mixed but generally credible. IQVIA materially beat 2021 guidance, landed adjusted profit within 2022 guidance despite an FX-driven reported revenue miss, modestly missed all three 2023 ranges, delivered revenue near the low end and EPS within range in 2024, and exceeded 2025 revenue guidance while delivering EBITDA and EPS within range. The company raised all three 2026 ranges after Q2.

Michael Fedock became CFO in February 2026 after leading finance for R&D Solutions and IQVIA Laboratories, replacing long-time CFO Ron Bruehlman. The internal succession supports continuity, though Fedock has a short public-company CFO record.

Governance is less compelling than operating continuity. Bousbib also chairs the board; a 2026 shareholder proposal to separate the roles received 24.8% support. The 2025 say-on-pay vote received 80.1% support. Bousbib's 2025 compensation was $28.1 million, and incentives emphasize revenue, adjusted EBITDA, adjusted EPS, cash flow, and relative TSR, without an explicit disclosed acquisition-ROIC gate. He beneficially owned approximately 1.4% of shares at January 31, 2026. It repurchased approximately $5.15 billion of shares during 2021-2025 and another $950 million in H1 2026. **CapEx.** Capital expenditure has been stable near $600-$675 million annually, supporting software, data infrastructure, laboratories, offices, and technology. It consumed roughly 22%-24% of operating cash flow in recent periods. TTM capex was $635 million.

**Debt.** Net debt increased from $10.76 billion at year-end 2021 to $13.74 billion at year-end 2025 and $14.09 billion at June 2026. Net leverage remained in a relatively narrow 3.3x-3.6x adjusted EBITDA range, but refinancing costs have risen. IQVIA priced $2 billion of 6.375% senior notes due 2034 on September 9, 2026, intended to refinance 5.0% notes and revolver borrowings. Higher interest expense directly reduced 2026 EPS guidance flexibility.

**Overall assessment.** Buybacks have demonstrably reduced the share count and were often executed below the current price. M&A may have strengthened the platform, but disclosed evidence is insufficient to prove attractive deal-level returns. The balance sheet can support the strategy while cash generation remains durable, but leverage narrows the margin for execution error.

## 7. Risks, Headwinds, & Catalysts

### Risks: Analyze key headwinds, including regulatory threats, competitive pressures, and industry-specific cyclicality

1. **Clinical cancellations and backlog conversion.** Trials can be delayed, reprioritized, resized, or canceled for scientific, strategic, funding, or operational reasons. Backlog is not guaranteed revenue. IQVIA's preliminary inactive-trial review estimated about 5% of backlog, but the review was unfinished at the research cutoff.
2. **Biotech and pharma spending cycles.** Emerging-biopharma funding is volatile and converts into awards with a lag. Large pharma can alter portfolios after clinical failures, patent changes, or drug-pricing policy.
3. **Pass-through and mix quality.** Reimbursed expenses inflate gross revenue without profit. Greater FSP, real-world services, or acquired-business mix can dilute margins even when revenue accelerates.
4. **Data access, privacy, and cybersecurity.** IQVIA depends on third-party data suppliers, permitted-use rights, de-identification, cross-border processing, and client trust. GDPR, U.S. state privacy rules, data localization, or supplier restrictions could raise cost or reduce product utility. A breach could damage the core franchise.
5. **AI disruption.** AI can improve trial design, recruitment, analytics, and productivity, but can also automate lower-level consulting and operational work, strengthen software competitors, or allow clients to internalize tasks. IQVIA has not disclosed standalone AI revenue or quantified net margin benefit.
6. **Competition and interoperability.** Large CROs have comparable global footprints; Veeva has meaningful CRM migration momentum; specialist data vendors can outperform in focused use cases. Clients may prefer interoperable best-of-breed stacks over an IQVIA-controlled platform.
7. **Leverage and refinancing.** June 2026 net debt was $14.09 billion and leverage 3.59x adjusted EBITDA. Higher rates and refinancing costs pressure EPS and constrain acquisition or buyback flexibility.
8. **Regulatory and policy risk.** Drug-pricing reform, IRA implementation, most-favored-nation policy, tariffs, FDA/NIH staffing or funding changes, and healthcare privacy rules can delay customer decisions. IQVIA also faces antitrust scrutiny for data-enhancing acquisitions, as shown by the blocked DeepIntent transaction.
9. **Execution and fixed-price risk.** Global clinical trials require complex site, labor, regulatory, and vendor coordination. Enrollment delays, wage inflation, scope errors, or quality failures can impair margin and reputation.
10. **Adjusted-accounting dependence.** The valuation case often relies on adjusted EPS and EBITDA, which exclude substantial recurring amortization and other costs. GAAP net margin has declined despite revenue growth.
11. **Governance and capital allocation.** Combined CEO/Chair roles, high compensation, continued acquisitions without disclosed ROIC, and debt-supported buybacks merit monitoring.
12. **Currency.** More than half of revenue is generated outside the Americas. Translation can materially alter reported revenue, guidance, and debt economics.

### Catalysts: Highlight any notable foreseen events on the horizon that could re-rate the stock

1. **Completion of the inactive-backlog review in Q3 2026.** Confirmation near the preliminary 5% estimate, with no effect on guidance or next-12-month conversion, would reduce a key overhang.
2. **December 2, 2026 Investor Day.** Management is expected to update medium-term growth, margin, AI, and capital-allocation targets. A credible organic-growth framework with less reliance on M&A could support a higher multiple.
3. **Record backlog conversion.** Q2 R&D backlog was $34.2 billion, with $9.2 billion expected to convert over 12 months. Sustained ex-pass-through R&D growth would validate the recovery.
4. **Biotech funding recovery.** Management cited sharply improved funding in early 2026. Conversion into high-quality, funded RFPs and awards could extend growth into 2027-2028.
5. **Commercial launch cycle.** More drug approvals and launches can lift patient solutions, analytics, engagement, and outsourced commercial work.
6. **AI monetization and productivity.** Evidence that agents generate incremental subscription/service revenue, improve trial delivery, or reduce cost without cannibalizing existing work would strengthen the moat narrative.
7. **Margin expansion.** Better full-service mix, normalized pricing, productivity, and reduced pass-through growth could restore adjusted EBITDA margin expansion.
8. **Deleveraging or lower rates.** Reduced net leverage and interest expense would improve GAAP and adjusted EPS quality and increase capital-allocation flexibility.
9. **Disciplined buybacks.** The remaining $2.819 billion authorization at June 30 provides material support if repurchases are executed below intrinsic value without increasing leverage.
10. **M&A proof points.** Better disclosure of acquired-business growth and returns, or successful integration of Cedar Gate and discovery assets, could improve confidence in the acquisition strategy.
